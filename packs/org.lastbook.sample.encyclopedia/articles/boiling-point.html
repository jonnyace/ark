Boiling point

The boiling point of a liquid is the temperature at which its vapour pressure equals the pressure of the surrounding air. At sea level, pure water boils at 100 degrees Celsius (212 degrees Fahrenheit).

Altitude

Air pressure falls as altitude rises, so water boils at a lower temperature in the mountains. At about 2,000 metres (6,500 feet) water boils near 93 degrees Celsius. Food takes longer to cook, and guidance for making water safe by boiling asks for a longer boil at high elevation.

Pressure cookers

A pressure cooker raises the pressure inside the pot, which raises the boiling point of water to about 120 degrees Celsius and cooks food faster.
